# Never an Adeyemi — Season 1 ten-episode outline

## Locked format

- **Episodes:** 10
- **Runtime:** approximately 10 minutes each
- **Season runtime:** approximately 100 minutes
- **Release plan:** Episodes 1–2 free discovery; Episodes 3–10 paid continuation
- **Setting:** contemporary Lagos and Ibadan, Nigeria
- **Genre:** family drama / legal suspense
- **Audience:** general adult audience; accessible, emotionally grounded suspense

The ten-episode version keeps the approved characters, central conflict and ending from the five-episode draft. The former episode movements are compressed and divided into shorter chapters. No new mythology or replacement protagonist is introduced.

## Season spine

Chief Olumide Adeyemi dies and leaves an unexpected codicil: his long-time driver and childhood friend, Obinna Obi, must be offered forty percent of the operating company. Obinna's daughter Ify discovers that the Adeyemi fortune began with an equal partnership between their fathers and may have been taken through a copied signature. Tobi Adeyemi, the one family member who still remembers the friendship, must decide whether loyalty means protecting the family or telling the truth. The surviving evidence is destroyed, but witnesses and the original agreement remain. Obinna accepts the shares and transfers them to Ify, leaving the family with a company that must be rebuilt honestly.

## Cliffhanger rule

Episodes 1–9 end on a changed choice, discovery, threat or betrayal—not an ordinary pause. Episode 10 resolves the season's ownership question while leaving the future of the company and the Adeyemi–Obi relationship open for a second season.

## Continuity locks

- The old company began as an equal partnership between Obinna Obi's father and Chief Adeyemi's father.
- The replacement deed used a copied signature and made the Adeyemi side sole owner.
- Emmanuel Afolabi was the young legal handler who saw the replacement deed and later drafted an admission letter for Chief.
- Chief's private evidence was kept in a locked box in his study. Mrs. Adeyemi gave it to Kunle and Yewande; they burned the letter and destroyed the deed.
- Afolabi's draft and testimony corroborate the original agreement and Obinna's account; they do not turn the historic ownership wrong into a criminal prosecution.
- Obinna accepts the forty-percent interest, then directs the transfer to Ify.
