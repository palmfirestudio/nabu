# Palmfire scriptwriting

## Purpose

Convert human story direction into a coherent screenplay that another agent or editor can produce. Keep the human’s premise and ending authoritative; use agents for structure, detail, dialogue, continuity and production planning.

Read [script-format.md](references/script-format.md) before writing a screenplay or scene packet.

## Required input

Accept a conversational brief, then normalise it into:

- title and logline;
- genre, tone and audience;
- setting and world rules;
- principal characters;
- each character’s want, need, fear and relationship;
- beginning, major turn and ending;
- theme or dramatic question;
- runtime and episode count;
- language, rating and boundaries;
- reference mood, without copying protected expression.

If a missing detail would change the story materially, ask one focused question. Otherwise make a conservative assumption and label it.

## Workflow

### 1. Protect the story direction

Extract the human’s premise, characters, setting, genre, intended audience and ending. Do not replace the ending or introduce a new protagonist, mythology or twist without clearly marking it as an optional proposal.

### 2. Create the story spine

Produce:

- logline;
- central dramatic question;
- protagonist want and need;
- opposition and stakes;
- beginning, escalation, midpoint, crisis and ending;
- character arcs;
- episode-by-episode movement;
- continuity risks;
- unresolved questions.

Stop for human approval before writing the full screenplay when the project is new.

### 3. Outline the episode

For a 30-minute episode, use six sequences of approximately five minutes. Treat sequences as dramatic units; do not force every sequence to be one five-minute scene. Divide each sequence into two to four scenes.

For each sequence, state:

- purpose;
- objective;
- obstacle;
- turn;
- emotional movement;
- transition to the next sequence;
- approximate runtime.

### 4. Write the screenplay

Use standard screenplay formatting:

- scene headings such as `INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT`;
- present-tense action;
- character cues and dialogue;
- parentheticals only when necessary;
- transitions only when they improve clarity;
- no unnecessary camera directions in the submission script.

Keep dialogue specific to character. Use action, silence and subtext. Avoid exposition that characters would not naturally say.

### 5. Build scene packets

For each scene, provide:

- sequence and scene IDs;
- runtime target;
- location and time;
- characters present;
- dramatic purpose;
- character objective;
- conflict;
- scene turn;
- visual style and lighting;
- camera language;
- dialogue and sound requirements;
- continuity dependencies;
- generation prompts and exclusions;
- required reference images;
- edit notes.

### 6. Run quality checks

Check:

- runtime plausibility;
- clear cause and effect;
- character and location continuity;
- names, ages, objects and injuries;
- who knows what and when;
- episode hook and ending;
- readable dialogue;
- visual producibility;
- rights, likeness, safety and music risks;
- whether any reference has been copied too closely.

## Output package

Return the work in this order:

1. assumptions and unresolved questions;
2. story spine;
3. episode outline;
4. screenplay;
5. continuity ledger;
6. sequence/scene breakdown;
7. visual production packets;
8. voice, music and sound requirements;
9. human approval checklist.

## Palmfire rules

- Preserve the human’s story direction.
- Prefer clarity over ornamental prose.
- Do not imitate a living writer’s exact style.
- Use public scripts to study structure and formatting, never to copy dialogue or scenes.
- Keep the screenplay separate from production prompts.
- Make every scene earn its place through objective, conflict or change.
- Do not publish or approve final material autonomously.
- Flag uncertainty instead of inventing a false fact.
