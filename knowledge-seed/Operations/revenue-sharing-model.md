# Palmfire revenue-sharing model

**Status:** Recommended pilot structure  
**Purpose:** Flexible, project-based revenue participation without giving away Palmfire ownership

This is a business-design document, not legal or tax advice. Before taking money from passive funders or advertising a public investment opportunity, have a Nigerian lawyer review the arrangement.

## 1. Core decision

Raphael owns Palmfire. Funding and creative participation do not automatically create ownership in Palmfire.

Use a separate, signed participation schedule for each series or major campaign. A person can participate in one project and sit out the next. They do not receive a permanent percentage merely because they participated once.

Call them **project participants** or **funding participants** until a lawyer confirms that “partners” is the correct legal description.

## 2. Use a project ledger

Every series gets its own ledger:

- Series name and release window
- Approved production budget
- Approved advertising budget
- Every contribution received
- Proof of payment
- What each contribution paid for
- Revenue received, by source
- Refunds, payment fees, taxes and chargebacks
- Payouts already made

Do not mix the revenue of one series with another series unless the project schedule expressly says so.

The simplest participation window is: **from the project funding close through six months after the project’s first release**. A participant who does not fund the next series has no participation in the next series.

## 3. The payout waterfall

Calculate the waterfall in this order every month:

1. **Gross receipts** — all YouTube, Palmfire, sponsorship and other cash actually received.
2. **Net receipts** — gross receipts minus payment-processor fees, refunds, chargebacks and taxes that must be remitted.
3. **Operating reserve** — hold back 10% of net receipts until Palmfire has a small working buffer. The reserve is still Palmfire money; it is not somebody’s personal payout.
4. **Approved-cost recovery** — repay documented project advances to the people who paid them, up to the amount actually contributed. If cash is insufficient, recovery rolls forward; it is not guaranteed.
5. **Distributable surplus** — what remains after the previous steps.
6. **Operator share** — 50% of the distributable surplus to Raphael for ownership, system-building, coordination and operations.
7. **Participation pool** — the remaining 50% of the distributable surplus.

The important distinction is that Raphael receives 50% of **distributable surplus**, not 50% of gross sales. Paying percentages from gross sales would eventually leave no money for refunds, taxes, production or the next release.

## 4. How the participation pool works

Use two separate sub-pools so cash and creative work are not forced into one arbitrary points system:

- **Creative and execution pool:** 40% of the participation pool. Mario and other approved contributors earn this through agreed deliverables.
- **Funding pool:** 60% of the participation pool. Cash funders share this according to their accepted contributions.

That means, when both pools are active:

- Raphael: 50% of distributable surplus
- Creative/execution contributors together: 20%
- Funding contributors together: 30%

These are starting defaults, not permanent promises. The project schedule can change the sub-pool percentages before a project begins.

## 5. Funding calculation

Use actual cash paid, not promises or verbal pledges.

For each funder:

Funding units = accepted contribution in naira

Then:

Funder payout = funding pool × (that funder’s units ÷ all eligible funding units)

Example:

- Funder A contributes ₦20,000
- Funder B contributes ₦20,000
- Funder C contributes ₦40,000
- Total eligible funding = ₦80,000

Their funding-pool percentages are 25%, 25% and 50%. If the funding pool is ₦153,000, their payouts from that pool are ₦38,250, ₦38,250 and ₦76,500.

Their documented contributions are handled separately in the approved-cost recovery step. In the example, their ₦20,000, ₦20,000 and ₦40,000 advances are repaid first when the project has enough cash.

## 6. Creative and execution calculation

Before production starts, create a short deliverables schedule:

- Script supervision
- Video generation and shot review
- Editing and sound
- Marketing and campaign management
- Platform and operations work

Assign each deliverable a percentage of the creative/execution pool. The percentage is earned only when the deliverable is accepted. If Mario is the only creative contributor, he receives the full creative pool. If several people contribute, split it according to the signed schedule.

Do not change the percentages after seeing the revenue. Changes require a written agreement before the next payout period.

## 7. What “in and out” means

Do not make a person’s rights disappear unpredictably every month. That creates disputes and punishes people who funded production early.

Use this rule instead:

- A person opts into a named series or campaign.
- Their accepted contribution or deliverables create participation units for that project.
- They share in that project’s defined revenue window.
- They can decline the next project with no penalty.
- If they contribute additional money later, the new amount is recorded as a new contribution and does not rewrite earlier payouts.

Monthly payouts are only the timing of payment. They do not reset the project’s contribution record.

## 8. Worked example

Assume one month of Series 1 produces:

- Gross receipts: ₦1,000,000
- Fees, refunds and taxes: ₦100,000
- Net receipts: ₦900,000
- Reserve at 10%: ₦90,000
- Approved advances awaiting recovery: ₦300,000

The calculation is:

- Cash after reserve: ₦810,000
- Advances recovered: ₦300,000
- Distributable surplus: ₦510,000
- Raphael’s 50% operator share: ₦255,000
- Participation pool: ₦255,000
- Creative/execution pool: ₦102,000
- Funding pool: ₦153,000

The funders also receive their documented principal recovery from the ₦300,000 recovery line. If the project has not earned enough cash, the unrecovered amount remains outstanding and the agreement must say whether it rolls forward or is treated as risk capital.

## 9. The one choice to make before accepting money

Every contribution must be labelled one of two ways:

### Recommended pilot: recoverable project advance

Palmfire records the contribution as a project cost advance. It is repaid from project cash before the profit split, but repayment is not guaranteed if the project never earns enough.

### Simpler but harsher: risk capital

The contribution is not separately repaid. The contributor receives only funding-pool participation. This is easier to administer but harder to sell to people funding tools and ads.

Do not mix the two labels informally. The receipt and participation schedule must state which one applies.

## 10. Monthly close process

At month-end:

1. Export Paystack, YouTube and other payment statements.
2. Reconcile the Palmfire bank account.
3. Record fees, refunds, taxes and approved expenses.
4. Update each project’s contribution ledger.
5. Calculate the waterfall.
6. Send every participant a one-page statement.
7. Pay within seven business days after the statement is accepted.

The statement should show gross receipts, deductions, reserve, cost recovery, distributable surplus, each pool, each person’s units and the final payment.

## 11. Legal guardrail

Do not publicly advertise a formula such as “send ₦20,000 and receive a percentage of Palmfire’s future revenue.” A passive cash contribution tied to future returns can raise Nigerian capital-market and crowdfunding issues. The SEC states that investment crowdfunding is regulated, that unregistered online investment schemes can be illegal, and that returns cannot be guaranteed in a crowdfunding offering. See the [SEC crowdfunding rules](https://sec.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Rules-on-Crowdfunding_210121.pdf) and the [SEC notice on unregistered online investment schemes](https://home.sec.gov.ng/for-investors/keep-track-of-circulars/public-notice-unregistered-online-investment-schemes/).

For the lean pilot:

- Keep participation private and project-specific.
- Do not promise a return.
- Cap each participant’s share at actual cash received.
- Keep signed schedules, receipts and payout statements.
- Ask a Nigerian commercial lawyer and accountant to review the first agreement.

## 12. Recommended starting configuration

For Series 1, use:

- Raphael: 50% of distributable surplus
- Creative/execution pool: 20% of distributable surplus
- Funding pool: 30% of distributable surplus
- 10% reserve before the split
- Recoverable project advances, unless a signed schedule says risk capital
- Monthly close and payout
- Six-month revenue window for the series

This gives Palmfire a stable operating share, rewards Mario for accepted creative work, lets funders enter and exit by project, and avoids pretending that a one-time ₦20,000 contribution creates permanent ownership.
